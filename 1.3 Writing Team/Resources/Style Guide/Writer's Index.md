# Narrative Terms :
#### The Road Ahead
Narrative exploration of various mental health states and rehabilitation in the lonely post-apocalyptic world shrouded by the [[#Fog|fog]]. 
#### Lakshmi
A young and optimistic [[#Postal Guild (aka. Guild Hall)|postal carrier]] - a blank slate - travelling from [[#Villages|village]] to village, empathizing with lived experiences and equipping herself with [[#Coping Strategy (aka coping mechanism)|coping strategies]] to process her trauma and learn more about herself. Her journey is one of pilgrimage, for deep personal understanding. 

She is in her early twenties and has recently finished her training to become a [[#Postal Guild (aka. Guild Hall)|postal carrier]]. She is very eager and enthusiastic about her job and very much looks forward to all of the new people she is going to meet. Lakshmi’s eagerness is used to connect people. 

[[Narrative Goals#Main Character (Lakshmi) General Writing Guidelines|See Main Character (Lakshmi) General Writing Guidelines]]
#### Villages (narrative)
Environments (or levels, see [[#Villages (technical)]]) which have a unique theme, dictated by its story and the central [[#Coping Strategy (aka coping mechanism)|coping strategy]] [[#Lakshmi]] will learn. 

In practice, [[#Villages (narrative)|village]] stories are small arcs that might involve and/or affect villagers’ life. Each [[#Villages|village]] is surrounded by the [[#Fog|fog]], which acts as a narrative device to push the player to complete all the quests for all [[#Memories]] and mail. Ideally, it should tell a story about the people of the new world ([[Narrative Goals#Post-Calamity|see Post-Calamity Lore]]) and delve into the lore (e.g. it might explore questions like food availability, transportation, flora, fauna, communication, etc.). 

As the [[#Lakshmi|player]] clears the [[#Villages|village]] story and the [[#Fog|fog]], [[#Lakshmi]] should be directly impacted by learning a [[#Coping Strategy (aka coping mechanism)|coping strategy]].
#### Memories
Self-contained stories that explore the life of a villager before the Calamity (20-25 years ago or so). It may or may not be related to the village story. These are the most loose-form narratives you might find in the game. Should also be the shortest (about 3-5 scenes). They can involve only cutscenes, or only gameplay, or a mix of both (but we love to hear new creative ideas!). Lakshmi can reflect in the game about it (e.g. through a journal reflection system), but it will not directly impact the gameplay mechanics or plot progression. The player does not need to clear all the memories to advance to the next location. 
#### Villagers
Villages are NPCs living in the new world (see more in [[Narrative Goals#Post-Calamity|Post-Calamity Lore]]). They might be part of quests (memories, villages, receivers of mail, etc.) or just comment general lore to the player. Their dialogue should change depending on the time of the day.

[[Narrative Goals#Dialogue General Writing Guidelines|See Dialogue General Writing Guidelines]]
#### Fog
A narrative and gameplay device to monitor player progression (see gameplay loop). It is the "antagonist" or negative force in the story, this is Disaster Fiction. It also served as a catalyst for calamity and the fog illness (see more in [[Narrative Goals#Calamity|Calamity Lore]]).

[[Narrative Goals#The Fog General Writing Guidelines|See Fog General Writing Guidelines]]
#### Fog illness: Greying
A condition that affected most of the population during the [[Narrative Goals#Calamity|Calamity]], appearing at the same time as the mysterious [[#Fog|fog]] during calamity. The people exposed were met with a terrible fever and cough.  Symptoms followed a consistent trajectory of severe fatigue, followed by uncharacteristic asociality (including extreme efforts of social revulsion, into mental rumination, and finally, a severely worsening asynchronous heartbeat.). When the ill would cough, more of the [[#Fog|fog]] would come bursting out of their lungs. This strange phenomenon accelerated the spread of the [[#Fog|fog]] and very soon, no city was left untouched.  A large number of people died from it.  No new cases of fog illness have been discovered. (see more in [[Narrative Goals#Calamity|Calamity Lore]]).
#### Coping Strategy (aka coping mechanism) 
A loose term used clinically to define a strategy to manage or tolerate a stressful and/or unpleasant experience. As such, any story talking about a lived experience (all memories and villages) will have a coping strategy. Gameplay-wise, coping mechanisms will be small *gameplay mechanics* Lakshmi will learn to complete village stories (e.g. learning assertiveness in Dolphin Bay). Realistically, players will learn many coping skills from various quests, but by design, we will stick to one coping strategy per village. Coping strategies will be used for future gameplay (see *notes on the latest game design updates*).
#### Postal Guild (aka. Guild Hall)
It refers to the Postal Guild space Lakshmi will always refer to when she arrives to a new village. There should always be a [[#Guild Master]]. 
#### Guild Master
The maître of the [[#Postal Guild (aka. Guild Hall)]] of the village. [[Writer's Index#Postal Guild (aka. Guild Hall)|The postal guild]] was established by people who were postal carriers in the old world before the disaster. Most of the older workers are still active (as it has only been fifteen years) and they generally serve in higher positions. Most chapter leaders are older people who helped to establish the guild. (see more in [[Narrative Goals#Post-Calamity|Post-Calamity Lore]])
#### Calamity
Calamity is the disaster bringing the [[#Fog]] and the [[#Fog illness Greying]]. (see more in [[Narrative Goals#Calamity|Calamity Lore]])
#### Fog events (aka. Fog creatures and Fog puzzles)
These are events in the game are thematically related to the [[#Fog]]. They should be spooky and signal danger, similar to the [[#Fog]]. They can all be given form without some connected story

# Technical Terms 

*see the relationships between terms at [[Writer Document Hierarchy]]*

#### Plot 
This is the main action of the game. Writing related to the plot concerns broad sweeping actions which will advance Lakshmi’s learning. Writing documents consistent of [[Plot Outline Template|plot outlines]] and [[Brainstorm Template|plot brainstorming]]. The plot is specific to the [[#Villages|village]] (or between village locations). ==It consists of a main plot (described by the main plot outline) and several side plots (described by side plot outlines). Unlike side-plots, the player must complete the main plot in order to advance. Typically side-plots have fewer acts.== 

(see examples of our [[Narrative Goals#Game Plots|Plots]])
#### Lore 
Background history of the world that may not be directly exposed to the player in the main plot. The primary written document for the lore is the ==*world bible which is soon to be produced*==. 

#### Villages (technical)
These are the broad settings where the action takes place. Village Description documents cover all of the plots, character and visual descriptions necessary to construct the village. (see [[#Villages (narrative)]]).

#### Acts 
Each act represents a *key beat* or *turning point* in the overall plot. If the plot was a typical story the act structure might be as follows: introduction, rising action, climax, conclusion. Acts are described by [[Act Outline Template|act outlines]] . 
#### Quests 
Quests are *tasks* that are assigned to the player, and often advance the act Writing for quests typically include the scenes involved as well as the initiation and completion conditions. Each quest is described in a [[Quest Outline Template|quest outline]]. 
#### Locations 
Locations are all the *settings* that make up the villages: all the homes, rooms, forests or dungeons where the action of the plot will take place. Unlike scenes which only exist during a player’s quest, locations are typically explorable by the player and thus have default interactables (such as loot) which remain tied to them. Locations are described in [[Location Outline Template|location descriptions]] . 
#### Scenes 
Scenes are the *core action* of each quest. Scenes are set at various locations, but will often alter the original locations to fit the scene. For instance, new quest items or characters may be introduced. These changes as well as the action of the scene make up the [[Scene Outline Template|scene outline]]. 
#### Characters 
Characters can be either player driven (PC) or non-player driven characters (NPCs). Characters are the agents who drive the action. Writing documents for a character consist of a [[Character Profile Template|character profile]] (background description, appearance description, schedule description and dialogue outline). 
#### Dialogue 
Dialogue is a kind of action where two or more characters exchange information. Dialogue writing consists of a dialogue document which covers all of the dialogue initiated by each [[Dialogue Template|dialogue]] option as well as future dialogue options for the player. 
#### Events 
Events are a kind of pre-programmed action which are triggered by the player character (often by entering a particular space). An example event may be, Lakshmi entering the chalet and being greeted by Grandpa Dan. Writing for events involves an [[Event Script Template|event script]] composed of action descriptions, event specific dialogue, and environment descriptions. 
#### Cut Scenes 
Cut scenes are preprocessed videos which the player watches at key intervals. For example, an opening cut scene which introduces “the fog”. Writing for cut scenes involves a [[Cut Scene Script Template|cut scene script]]. 
#### Interactables
Actions that are triggered by the player interacting with an object in a scene. Typically interactables trigger player dialogue or the player’s inventory. Writing for interactables involves an [[Interactable Template|interactable]]  document. 
# Additional Technical Terms 

#### Logic Tags
These are small descriptions present in many of the writer documents. They keep track of the game state. For instance, if Lakshmi learns a big secret, the logic tags would record, “Lakshmi_has_learned_a_big_secret=True” 

#### Triggers
These are the player circumstances which cause something to occur. Typically triggers for an event will be entering a space (eg. Lakshmi enters the office). 
  
#### Clear
Clear refers to when a player completes something (a quest, dialogue etc.). Typically, clearing a quest will result in some kind of logic tag
