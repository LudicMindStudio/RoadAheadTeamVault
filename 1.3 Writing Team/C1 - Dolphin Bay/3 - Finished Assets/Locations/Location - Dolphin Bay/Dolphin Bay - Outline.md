# Dolphin Bay — Outline

> World bible entry for the first village. This is the trunk doc: the town's identity, history, themes, and story. Everything else expands off it — the scene outlines, the home outlines, the POIs, the location descriptions. This is not an asset that dissolves into game assets. It is the place the game is about.

## The One-Line

Dolphin Bay is a small coastal fishing town tucked into a semi-tropical bay shaped like a dolphin, rebuilt by people who survived the Calamity together, warm on the surface with the fog's scars underneath.

---

## Meta Purpose — The First Village

- The player's first village. Everything the game teaches happens here first: mail delivery, the fog, memories, coping strategies, relationships
- Lakshmi graduates here and learns her first coping strategy: **assertiveness**
- Sets the template for every future village: each village carries one mental health journey, one coping strategy, one face of the fog
- The village plot fully resolves by the end — Dolphin Bay reaches "normal or ideal operation" ([[Narrative Goals]])
- Pacing is faster here. It is the introduction to the whole game
- Establishes the game's core contrast: cozy world vs heavy fog. The town is the cozy; the fog at the edges is the reminder it is recovering, not recovered

---

## Themes

What Dolphin Bay endows into the game. Every location, home, and story beat in this town should serve at least one of these.

- **Marine identity** — a fishing town, not a produce exporter. The sea is the source of life, work, and memory. Lean into it
- **Community as survival** — the people who knew each other survived. The town runs on relationships, not institutions
- **The fog as emotional mirror** — anger feeds fog, fog feeds anger. In Dolphin Bay: anger → fog → dolphins leave → more anger ([[Narrative Goals]])
- **Warmth through struggles** — melancholy hopeful. Safe enough to feel hard things. Cozy interiors, gentle light, real grief under them
- **Isolation → belonging** — the town's arc (and the game's): people finding each other. Francois washed ashore alone, now the town's host. Garp's grief, Warren's lost Alfred, the kids holding it together
- **Honesty without sugarcoating** — recovery is slow, help is hard, some problems have no clean solution. The game says this plainly here
- **The dolphins** — symbol of what was saved, what is being lost, and what the town refuses to lose. Fin saved Francois; her absence breaks him
- **Youth as the moral compass** — the Kid Troop overhears the adults, plays through the cracks, and points at what the town pretends not to see

## History

### Pre-Calamity

- A close coastal fishing town. Garp's family fished these waters. Warren, Greg, Alfred, and Lisa grew up together here
- But the town had a second identity: a place people came to when they were soul-searching, trying to figure out their way in life. There was a yoga master, others like her. "Fishermen plus hippies" — fishing roots with a rag-tag community of city folk who came to find themselves
- The fishing industry was already going under before the Calamity
- The sanctuary building already stands at the water's edge — old, pre-Calamity ([[Dolphin Bay Sanctuary - Location Outline]]). Anita and Gyo have always been there, running a remote research lab on dolphin intelligence and communication, largely separate from the town's affairs
- People knew each other by name. That is what saved them

### The Calamity (15 years ago)

- The fog rolled in. The Greying illness followed. Society crumbled ([[Narrative Goals]])
- Compared to other places, the Calamity did not change Dolphin Bay absurdly. It kept its purpose: a place people come to when they need shelter, or to find themselves
- In Dolphin Bay: ships were lost. Francois's cargo ship hit fog, exploded, and sank — a dolphin named Fin pulled him to shore. He lost his arm and, as far as he knows, his whole crew
- Alfred was lost to the Calamity — Warren has searched for him ever since
- Cat was born as the world ended. Alexi died in childbirth. Garp has her ashes, waiting to be scattered at sea
- The town survived because it was small and knew itself — the rural resilience the GDD describes

### Post-Calamity (the rebuild)

- Survivors coalesced and rebuilt. The guild chapter was founded, with Greg as its leader — the connective tissue of the new world
- The town kept drawing people who needed a place: a rag-tag community gathered for shelter, continuing the old soul-searching purpose
- Juliette and the villagers found Francois on the shore and nursed him back. He dedicated his life to his new home
- The sanctuary kept its research. Anita and Gyo stayed, separate from the village, studying the dolphins. Fin was raised there as a young dolphin — that is why he is special to Francois
- The town rebuilt by hand: the sea wall, the fish market, homes in the forest. Handmade, imperfect, warm
- Fifteen years later the world has healed to a livable state. Day-to-day life is cozy. The scars are underneath

### Present Day

- The dolphins are leaving. Fin stopped showing up — she is pregnant, and Francois does not know it
- Francois's psychosis has taken root around her absence. The protest has grown. The sanctuary is blamed
- The cycle is spinning: anger → fog → dolphins leave → more anger
- Garp's catches are thinning. The village is worried, and that worry is being weaponized
- The town's anxiety made visible: the protest at the statue, the empty nets, the fog rolling in off the bay

## The Town Today

- **Identity** — fishing town, animal lovers, handmade and imperfect. Bright buildings, thin walls, plenty of windows. Grass growing through the stone
- **Daily life** — morning on the sea, afternoon rounds through town, evening in the homes. Schedules are the town's heartbeat ([[TRA Meeting Aug 10th 2026]])
- **The sea wall** — stone bricks along the coast, stairs at several points, locals sit there to watch the water
- **The bay** — dolphin-shaped, semi-tropical. The water used to be clear enough to see sealife; it is not so nice anymore
- **What the town believes** — the fog is a fact of life. The sanctuary debate is the current wound
- **Animal life** — dolphins (leaving), turtles (Sammy's care), the sanctuary's land animals, birds resting on the bridge

## Geography & Layout

From the location brainstorm ([[Dolphin Bay Locations]]) and the art map sketches ([[Dolphin Bay (Eastern side)]]).

- The bay is shaped like a dolphin, opening to the ocean at the tail
- The town square sits at the tail-end, where the bay meets the ocean. The water leaving the bay splits the town in two halves; a small island with bridges connects them
- **Guild Hall** — centre, on the bisecting island. Ornamented, prideful, the town's centrepiece ([[Greg - Profile]])
- **East side** — the fish market (a few stalls around the dolphin statue fountain), the dolphin statue (the village symbol, where the protesters gather), the kid's playground (close enough that the kids overhear the protesters), the garden area (Shirley's beds, the gazebo, a shed and well)
- **West side** — residences and the inn
- **Sea wall** — along the coast, stone and brick, ascended by stairs
- **The forest** — hardy and hilly beyond the beach layer, sparse enough to run through, clearings form the paths. Most dwellings are in the forest, not on the beach
- **The docks** — around the centre island's circumference. Smaller vessels only. No large ships in a long while ([[Garp - Profile]])
- **The sanctuary** — outside town, down a small path. A barn-house opening onto the water, buoys marking the boundary, fences on the land side ([[Dolphin Bay Sanctuary - Location Outline]])
- **Greg's Chalet** — out of town on a forested cliff side, view of the waterfront, a small rocky beach below. Where Lakshmi lives ([[Greg's Chalet - Location Outline]])
- **Coastal path** — follows the shore up toward the mountain range. Bubble Town lies along that mountain

## The Village Story

Two quest lines run through Dolphin Bay, and both fully resolve before the player leaves:

1. **The protests** — Francois's movement against the sanctuary, fed by his psychosis and the town's fear
2. **The secrets of the dolphins** — why they are leaving, what happened to them, the erased history (Fin's disappearance, the empty pens, Dr. Lamborghini's monitoring equipment)

The cycle the story must break: anger → fog → dolphins leave → more anger.

Mystery seeds from the early brainstorms:
- The disappeared man and the letter addressed to "Hustle" ([[Dolphin Bay - Storyline 2]])
- The erased history of the dolphins — does it connect to the Fog? ([[Dolphin Bay - Storyline 2]])
- Warren's Alfred sightings in the fog ([[Warren - Profile]])
- What the kids overhear at the protest podium ([[Dolphin Bay (Eastern side)]])

The resolution state: the town reaches normal or ideal operation. Understanding weakens the fog. The dolphins begin to return (post-story state in [[Dolphin Bay Sanctuary - Location Outline]]).

## The Fog in Dolphin Bay

- Environmental manifestation — it rolls in, it affects wildlife, it feeds on the protesters' anger. No creatures here; this town's fog is atmosphere
- Where the town's fear is dense, the fog is heavy: the protest, the docks with empty nets, the forest's edge where Warren watches for Alfred
- The fog here is the town's anxiety made visible. Empathy and understanding thin it; panic and anger thicken it
