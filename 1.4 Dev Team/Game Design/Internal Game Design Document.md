
## What is game design?
Game design is the clearest way to encapsulate what playing the game is like. When someone asks "Whats your game like?", we can explain in a few ways, like through themes and mechanics (RPG, platformer, etc), through a basic use case explaining the actions the player will take, or even just anecdotal/vibes based explanations.

When any of us are making anything for the game, we are in some way designing it. We talk about game design so that we have a mutual goal and understanding for what the player will end up experiencing. 

Game design is not static and it is not scope exhaustive. We all are going to have our own understanding of the game design, that will constantly align and refine with each other. Pillars of design can be interpreted in different ways by different people and will need to be clarified by digging deeper. 


## Zinger

The Road Ahead is a game that follows Lakshmi's journey as she grows as a person and learns about the world. The recovering post apocalyptic world is full of stories for the player to experience on the road to becoming a better person and overcoming adversary. By being helpful to the people of the new world, Lakshmi and the player are able to see the changes caused by their actions. 

## Our Game Design Pillars

Before anything, do remember that optimal game design is not the goal of the [[Project Design Note|Project Design]]. While we design around our pillars of game design, priority and flexibility is given in way of the project goals when the two are misaligned.

### Lakshmi
We are making a role playing game. Its an escape and a fantasy. Its not a simulation or a game to maximise a score. The player experiences the game through Lakshmi and events are based on what the player does as Lakshmi. 

### Storytelling
We are using the medium of video games to tell stories. We use game design to tell these stories interestingly and fill in the gaps to complete the experience. Routine skill based gameplay does not gate the progression of the game and the stories we are trying to tell.

### Lovable world
For many reasons in the project, the world that we are making needs to be interesting for the player to be in. Mechanics, events, and design effort is used to have the space the player is in feel dynamic, real, and enjoyable to simply be in. Simply walking around the world, let alone participating in it, should have a constant feel of discovery, relateability, and belonging.

### Player Reflection
We want our game to make the player think. Thinking about the events that are happening in the game and compare them to their own experiences and beliefs. 

## What makes our game unique

- Memories are short experiences from real world youth. They are a evergreen and continuous mechanic. New Memory NPCs or Villages can arise within the development and post release of the game. These stories can be self contained with little to do with the themes and topics pre and post entering the memory. 
- Player checkins. The Road Ahead cares about the player as a person. The game does not shy away from speaking to the player directly, superseding Lakshmi as the players avatar. This is done to meet the player where they are at, enhancing the range of topics and themes while keeping the tone of the game approachable and helpful. Otherwise, mechanics allow for the player reflect their own state onto the game, and experiment with the change of themselves.
- We empower youth. We respect the position of youth in the societies that we have and embrace capturing whats important in this stage of life. We cater to our audience and priorities being understood by those who need it versus everyone.
## The Fantasy

When playing our game, the players are expecting something. This expectation can be shaped by our communication, advertising, etc. At any given point, we should aim to either meet/exceed the expectations of the player. Or we change their expectation in a way that they enjoy (hard).

Our game is about mental health and Lakshmi delivering mail. Along with story elements, game design elements should also support this. If we are ever designing something that does not complete this fantasy, then that design is viewed under the lens as taking a break from this fantasy (thus reinforcing it).


## Basic Gameplay Constants

### The Fog
The fog is our antagonist. It does bad things. It embodies negative mental health to cause obstructions, problems, and challenges for the player. The fog is a natural force and any further characterisation is not constant. The fog can manifest into almost anything for the relevant situation. The fog can exist anywhere, but always exists in the unknown. 

Travelling in-between villages and POIs puts the player into dense fog. When not directly tied to a narrative beat, the fog shows its antagonistic force through random challenges for the player to overcome. 

### Mail Delivery
Lakshmi is a postal worker in the new world. Delivering mail is her job and she will do it often. Most of Lakshmi's objectives are in someway nested to doing her job, and we can always expect the player to be able to simply deliver mail. 

### Npc relations
The NPCs in TRA are alive and dynamic. They change as things happen in the game. This includes but is not limited to, world state changes (day, season, time), interactions with the player, interactions with other npcs, interactions with the environment. The player noticing or wanting to interact with changing these NPCs, allows the player to effect the world as a result of their actions.

The player can develop relationships with NPCs increasing a relationship meter. This could be increased by simply visiting the NPC often or delivering them mail, along with other specifics. This would effect the NPC giving them more dialogue to say or unlocking certain quests, along with other specifics. 

### Memories 
As mentioned, memories are isolated. But they are given space and gameplay incentives for completing them that are mostly decoupled from the content of the memory itself. 

Memories are connected to Lakshmi's power, the ability to connect to those with emotions to share. The manifestation of Lakshmi's ability and her relationship to it change throughout the game.

### Lakshmi's Journal
Many tracking and reflective elements can be found in Lakshmi's journal. This includes her (the player's) thoughts and feelings towards NPCs, events, and memories. 


# Gameplay loops

A gameplay loop is a bit of connected gameplay decisions that ends up being repeated many times throughout the game. Most gameplay loops are nested into other gameplay loops and can all fall under a central loop.

Our main root loop is simple, navigating the world as lakshmi. This loop is the RPG. 

We also have the memory loop, which is a more simplified version of the main navigation of the game.


## Main Loop

Being an RPG, the main loop of the game follows the main quest of the game and the story. To complete the entire game, the player needs to complete the main quest. The main quest branches down in to sub quests in each village and other events that make up Lakshmi's journey.

As lakshmi progresses down her journey, mechanically she can change in two ways

**Coping Strategies** are abilities that Lakshmi gains throughout her story. Coping Strategies are few and can be thought of like permanent power ups. They give the player access to something or perform actions they couldn't before. 

**Inventory** Lakshmi has an inventory that she can use for mail and other items. Mail and key items will simply take space while other items can be used to produce some effect

The inventory itself is precious space. the player is always trying to manage it effectively and not have it overfilled. They must balance mail, along with other items that they may want. 
## Subloop 1 - Mail delivery 

The Mail delivery loop is ever-present in the game and services as a jumping off point for other quests and systems. We can weave in important narrative mail to deliver while keeping a repeatable objective for the player to do.

Mail delivery itself is very simple. Lakshmi picks up mail at the guild hall and delivers it to the intended recipient. Everyday there is new mail to be delivered so the player can do choose to simply spend their time delivering mail. The recipient can be randomly chosen, or weighted to certain characters for story purposes.

There can be different mail types to be delivered, such as:
- Different inventory sizes
- Urgent mail that must be delivered in a set amount of time
- In person mail that must be delivered to the person
- House delivery mail that can be delivered to a mailbox or door

Once the mail is delivered, the player interacts with that npc and it counts as an NPC interaction.  

By delivering up to certain thresholds of mail, Lakshmi's postal guild position will rise, unlocking small upgrades as her badge increases.

We encourage the player to know the villages, both the maps and the npcs who live in them. its likely we will need some sort of helper system for making this task easier. but its important it isnt too easy. understanding the NPCs and their routines is the gameplay here. 


#### Design Skill
To make the mail delivery basics interesting, it really depends on clever level design and interesting NPC schedules. Players will be planing a "route" for how they want to deliver their mail and go about their day. If the player doesnt have enough knowledge of the route, then they will budget time for each mail item. 

Emergent playstyles and strategies can arise. Maybe the player waits until all the NPCs go to the pub in the evening to deliver a bunch of mail at once? maybe they needed to go in a direction and know that an npc lives that way? 
## Subloop 2 - Fog exploration
While mail delivery within a certain town is a peaceful and exploratory time, delivering between towns or simply travelling between towns introduces another layer of complexity. Navigating the fog between villages has the player enter "Fog challenge rooms". The rooms must be cleared before they can move on. a certain number of fog challenge rooms are encountered in a row depending on the destination. 

The type of fog room and its challenge are random and change every time the player enters a new fog room. To clear basic rooms, no preparation is required and the player must complete the skill based challenge to clear the room. This can involve dispatching of fog monsters or clearing a fog barrier through so other means. Different rooms can appear based on the coping strategies that Lakshmi has. Various items can be used to help ease the fog challenge in some way.

Other then puzzle platforming, one new solid mechanic is needed here.

# Minigames

We have a lot of room for "minigames" or abstractions of a complex tasks into a isolated game. These need to be careful not to become a slog or break in the flow the player is getting into.

Here are some opportunities for minigames:

## Mail sorting

Before mail can be delivered or as a separate process in the Guild hall, mail can be sorted. This manifests as simple categorization/matching style game.

The basic premise is:
- unsorted mail comes in
- based on each mail some randomly assigned unsorted info can be found. these can include
	- Destination village
	- Priority
	- package vs mail
	- package weight
- Based on the random assignment of the mail tasks will need to be done per item. these can include
	- Marking the mail
	- Sorting it into a certain destination
- Once the mail has all been sorted, a "score" is assigned 

The mail sorting can then be a requirement before the mail is picked up to be delivered.

**References**
Think of "*Papers Please*". in that game, you must scan through documents from immigrants and then decide if they should enter the country or not. the player must carefully check the info to make sure they made the right choice. In our game, the info needs to be checked correctly to assign it in the right final bin
## Memory Entrance

Before a memory is entered a minigame is played. This would be a simple puzzle based around the brain.

the basic premise is:
- a graph (nodes and edges) is presented as neurons of the brain
- at bottom nodes of the graph signals can be sent
- signals travel up the graph following some basic rules to determine which edge to travel.
- after each edge the player can either wait or send another signal starting at another node
- the objective is to have all nodes on the other side of the graph all light up at once

**References**
The gameloop idea stems from "*To The Moon*" where in you must solve a basic grid puzzle to move on to the next "memory".

The idea for the puzzle stems from the basic of a puzzle found in *Baldurs Gate 3* where in different neurons need to be activated to follow along a stem to reach its corosponding emotion
